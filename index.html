What is the Stage Gate Process?

A structured sequence of stages and decision points that every IT project must pass through — from the initial idea to production closure. Each gate is a formal review where evidence is assessed and a Go / Kill / Hold / Recycle decision is made. No stage can begin until the previous gate has formally passed.

Exit Point A

Before Stage 1

Department has its own capable team. They self-manage the project but must still follow central governance: security policies and architecture standards.

Exit Point B

After Stage 3 — Requirements done

BAPS BAs help the department define requirements, then the department exits to issue an RFP to an external vendor for the build phase.

Exit Point C

After Stage 5 — Estimate done

IT's estimate exceeds the department's timeline or budget. The department finds an external solution. IT is never pressured to rush and compromise quality.

Industry Context — Cooper Stage-Gate® vs BAPS 8-Stage

The BAPS model is grounded in Cooper's Stage-Gate® framework (the global industry standard) and extended for a volunteer nonprofit IT organization.

Cooper Stage-Gate® (Industry Standard)

1
DiscoveryIdentify opportunities, generate ideas
2
ScopingQuick preliminary assessment
3
Build Business CaseDetailed investigation, market research, technical feasibility
4
DevelopmentBuild the solution
5
Testing & LaunchValidate and deploy

BAPS 8-Stage Model

1
Project ProposalMaps to Discovery + early Scoping. ITC/BOD gate.
2
Project InitiationMaps to Scoping — formalizes team and governance.
3
RequirementsMaps to Build Business Case — BRD + FRD split is a BAPS extension.
4–5
Technical Design + EstimateCooper combines these; BAPS separates design from costing — reduces estimation surprises.
6–9
Dev + UAT + Launch + ClosureMaps to Testing & Launch. BAPS adds formal Closure and Lessons Learned loop.

BAPS Extensions Beyond Cooper

BRD / FRD Split
Cooper treats requirements as one stage. BAPS splits BRD (what/why) from FRD (how) with a formal gate between them — preventing design from starting on incomplete logic.
Exit Points A / B / C
Cooper assumes IT always builds. BAPS provides three structured off-ramps that give departments flexibility while preventing IT burnout from unrealistic expectations.
Readiness Overlap Model
Cooper gates are sequential. BAPS stages overlap: prerequisites for the next stage begin while the current stage is running, preventing stalls (e.g., finding a Solution Architect before Stage 3 ends).
Volunteer RACI
Cooper assumes dedicated staff. BAPS RACI accounts for limited volunteer availability, rotating team members, and the need to distribute accountability across a semi-professional governance structure.
Formal Closure Stage
Cooper's model ends at Launch. BAPS adds Project Closure with formal documentation archival, access revocation, and a Lessons Learned loop that feeds back into process improvements.
Defect Severity Classification
Cooper focuses on business gates. BAPS adds a P0–P3 defect severity framework to formalize what must be resolved before UAT sign-off, preventing "go-live with known issues" disputes.

⚠ Critical Gaps — Must Fix Before Publishing

CRITICAL
C1 · No project size tiering
BAPS: A one-day enhancement and a multi-year platform both walk the same four ITC-reviewed gates. Industry (COBIT / Stage-Gate): 2–3 tiers are standard — Lite (single gate, IT-approved, <defined cost threshold), Standard (full PTX), and Major (BAPS Inc. leadership required).
Recommendation: Define three tiers by cost + effort (e.g., Lite: <$5K / <40h; Standard: $5K–$50K; Major: >$50K or org-wide). Lite uses a single combined gate with IT-only approval. Without this, Departments will route around the policy entirely.
CRITICAL
C2 · ITC has no decision SLA
BAPS: Departments have obligations at every gate; ITC has none — no meeting cadence, no turnaround commitment. Industry (Stage-Gate model): Gate reviews are SLA-bound — typically ≤10 business days from complete submission to decision, with a "silence = no objection" rule for advisory inputs and auto-escalation on slippage.
Recommendation: Add: ITC meets [bi-weekly/monthly]. Decision due within one ITC cycle of a complete submission. If no decision in [X] days, auto-escalates to BAPS Inc. leadership. This becomes the most cited complaint within 6 months if left unaddressed.
CRITICAL
C3 · No Kill / Redirect decision mechanism
BAPS: §12 says non-compliant projects "may be paused, deferred, or returned." Industry: Every gate has four explicit outcomes: Go (proceed + release next funding tranche), Kill (stop, document rationale), Hold (pause with defined re-entry criteria), Redirect (scope/approach change). Kill decisions are tracked and celebrated — they prevent sunk-cost projects.
Recommendation: Add a Gate Decision Record template to each gate section with Go / Kill / Hold / Redirect as checkboxes, rationale field, conditions, owners, and target dates. Require ITC to document which outcome and why at every gate.
CRITICAL
C4 · No benefits realization / post-implementation review
BAPS: §7 requires expected benefits at intake — but nothing checks whether they materialized. Industry (COBIT BAI11 Gate E): Closure gate is mandatory: acceptance sign-off, lessons learned, and benefits tracking approach. PMI best practice adds a formal post-implementation review at 30/60/90 days.
Recommendation: Add Gate 5: Permit to Close — 60-day post-launch review covering actual vs. expected benefits, support model handoff confirmation, lessons learned filed with IT, and ITC sign-off. This closes the feedback loop that improves future estimates.
CRITICAL
C5 · Volunteer capacity is unmodeled (BAPS-specific)
BAPS: The policy assumes assignable IT resources with predictable capacity. In practice, a significant share of delivery capacity is likely volunteer. Nothing covers resource unavailability mid-build or realistic capacity forecasting at Gate 4.3. Industry: Even commercial organizations require a Resource Management Plan at the Permit to Build gate — this gap is more acute for BAPS.
Recommendation: Add a volunteer capacity acknowledgment: "Estimates at Gate 4.3 will account for volunteer availability and seasonal constraints. If a key resource becomes unavailable, the PM must notify ITC within [X] days and submit a revised resource plan or change request."

● High Gaps — Address Before First Project Runs Through

HIGH
H1 · No RACI matrix
BAPS: §11 lists roles but with narrative descriptions. Industry (COBIT): A gate-by-gate RACI (Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed) is required — it resolves ambiguous ownership at a glance, especially the IT vs. Department support question and who approves rollout.
Recommendation: Add Appendix A: RACI table with rows = key activities per gate and columns = ITC, IT RGC, IT PM, BA, Dept SME, BAPS Inc. Leadership. Resolves gaps C3, H1, and the rollout approver inconsistency in one artifact.
HIGH
H2 · Vendor / third-party procurement is absent
BAPS: Many IT projects will be SaaS or vendor-delivered — the policy says nothing about it. Industry: Gate 2 must include vendor selection criteria, contract/licensing review, data processing agreement, security assessment of the vendor, and data portability/exit terms.
Recommendation: Add to §4.2 checklist: "Where a third-party vendor is involved, Gate 4.2 must include vendor evaluation, data classification review, contract/DPA review, and cybersecurity assessment." Add a Vendor Clause to §3 Governance Principle.
HIGH
H3 · No emergency / event-driven fast path
BAPS: §12 allows exceptions "approved by ITC" with no criteria or timeline. Festival and event deadlines are real and immovable. Industry: A defined compressed path (e.g., Gates 1–3 combined into a single 48-hour review with senior approval) prevents the policy from being bypassed routinely once one project slips through.
Recommendation: Add §4.5 Emergency / Event-Driven Path: criteria (e.g., event <30 days, scope <defined threshold), compressed combined gate, named approvers (ITC Chair + IT RGC), post-event retrospective required. Track usage to detect policy bypass patterns.
HIGH
H4 · P0 classification has no consequence defined
BAPS: §8 explains how ITC decides, but not what P0 gets you — does it skip gates? Jump the queue? Get dedicated resources? Industry: Priority classifications must define the operational consequence: dedicated resources, compressed gate timeline, board-level visibility, or all three. Without consequence, the label is meaningless.
Recommendation: Add to §8: "A P0-classified project receives: [dedicated IT resource allocation / combined gate review within 5 business days / weekly ITC status update / BAPS Inc. leadership visibility]. P0 does not bypass cybersecurity or UAT gates." Also add P1/P2/P3 tiers below P0.
HIGH
H5 · Rollout approver is inconsistent with other gates
BAPS: Gates 4.1–4.3 are approved by ITC. Gate 4.4 (Rollout) is approved jointly by IT RGC + Department, with ITC absent. Industry: The go-live gate (Gate D) typically requires the same governance body that approved the prior gates to sign off, with the operational body (IT RGC equivalent) providing readiness certification as input — not as the approver.
Recommendation: Clarify whether the omission of ITC at Gate 4.4 is deliberate (ITC trusts IT RGC + Dept at this stage) or an oversight. Document the rationale either way. If deliberate, define what triggers ITC re-engagement at rollout (e.g., scope change >X% during build).
HIGH
H6 · Support tier model is undefined
BAPS: §3 says business-side support sits with Departments; §4.4 says IT transitions to a "production support group" after hypercare. HIGH

BAPS: §8 gives ITC sole discretion; no route for a Department that disagrees with a gate decision. Industry: Every governance framework requires a defined escalation path — typically to the next level of authority (board or leadership) with a time-bound response and documented rationale requirement.
Recommendation: Add to §8 or §12: "A Department that disagrees with an ITC gate decision may escalate in writing to BAPS Inc. Leadership within [10] business days. Leadership will review and respond within [one leadership cycle]. ITC's decision remains in effect pending escalation review."
