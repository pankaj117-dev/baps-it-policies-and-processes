Key Governance Rules

Project Tiering
Lite (<$5K / <40h): 3-stage fast track, IT RGC approval only.
Standard ($5K–$50K): Full 9 stages + all PTX gates, ITC approval.
Major (>$50K / org-wide): Full process + BAPS Inc. Leadership co-approval.
Decision SLAs
ITC meets bi-weekly. Gate decisions: Lite ≤5 days, Standard ≤10 days, Major ≤15 days. Emergency path: ≤48 hours. SLA clock starts on complete submission.
Every Gate: Go / Kill / Hold / Recycle
Every PTX gate and every Stage Gate produces a written Gate Decision Record. Kill and Hold decisions are governance successes — tracked and used in annual policy review.
Change Control
Cost >10% of last approved baseline → ITC notification. >20% → gate re-review. Schedule slip >4 weeks → CR required. All tracked cumulatively.
Emergency Fast Path
Festival/event deadline ≤30 days + Tier 1/2 scope + pre-documented risks → Gates 1–4 combined into a single 48-hour review. Rollout + Closure gates still required.
Benefits Realization
60-day post go-live review comparing actual vs. promised KPIs. Required for PTX-5 (Permit to Close). Lessons fed back into process improvement annually.

What is the Stage Gate Process?

A structured sequence of stages and decision points that every IT project must pass through — from the initial idea to production closure. Each gate is a formal review where evidence is assessed and a Go / Kill / Hold / Recycle decision is made. No stage can begin until the previous gate has formally passed.

Exit Point A

Before Stage 1

Department has its own capable team. They self-manage the project but must still follow central governance: security policies and architecture standards.

Exit Point B

After Stage 3 — Requirements done

BAPS BAs help the department define requirements, then the department exits to issue an RFP to an external vendor for the build phase.

Exit Point C

After Stage 5 — Estimate done

IT's estimate exceeds the department's timeline or budget. The department finds an external solution. IT is never pressured to rush and compromise quality.

§1 · Purpose▾

As BAPS Swaminarayan Sanstha continues to expand, the demand for IT application development, system enhancements, automation, reporting, integrations, and digital solutions is steadily increasing. This growing demand necessitates the formalization and strengthening of the IT project intake and PTX processes to ensure all requests are consistently evaluated, appropriately prioritized, and executed with clear governance, accountability, and alignment of resources.

The PTX process introduces defined approval gates that a project must pass through as it progresses from proposal to requirements, design, development, and rollout. These control points ensure alignment with business value, available capacity, risk considerations, budget expectations, and Sanstha-wide priorities.

Definition: PTX (Permit to Execute) — the formal authorization required to proceed to the next approved stage of a project.
§2 · Scope▾
IN SCOPE
  • New IT application development projects
  • New system requests
  • Major enhancements to existing systems
  • New phases or major features of existing projects
  • Critical enhancement requests from Departments
  • Project change requests impacting scope, timeline, cost, resources, or delivery
  • Production rollout readiness
OUT OF SCOPE

Production bugs, incidents, service disruptions, or outages — these continue under IT RGC incident management procedures.

In-flight projects: Projects not yet in build join at their next natural gate. Projects in build adopt §§11–14 immediately. Projects in UAT or hypercare complete under existing arrangements.
§3 · Governance Principles▾
IT owns delivery — all resources (dev, architecture, BA, PM, testing, cybersecurity, infrastructure, deployment) managed by IT.
Departments own the business — direction, SME input, UAT, training, sign-off. Departments do not manage IT resources.
Cybersecurity throughout — controls required at design, build, testing, and rollout stages.
Clear roles and escalation — protects the Sanstha from uncontrolled scope, unclear ownership, and delivery risks.

⚠ Critical — Most Likely to Break the Process

GAP A No size tiering (v1.0) RESOLVED IN V0.3 §5

A small project and an org-wide platform both go through four gates with ITC review at each. Departments will route around it. The policy will be seen as too heavy for small work and will be quietly ignored for anything that feels minor.

Recommendation: Implement a 2–3 tier model: Lite (single combined gate, IT-approved, below a cost/effort threshold), Standard (full 4-gate PTX), and Major (full PTX + BAPS Inc. leadership co-approval).
GAP B ITC has no decision SLA (v1.0) RESOLVED IN V0.3 §6

Departments have obligations at every gate; ITC has none. Without a committed turnaround time, "the policy is slowing us down" becomes the standing complaint — and it would be valid.

Recommendation: Define ITC meeting cadence, a decision turnaround (e.g. within one ITC cycle of a complete submission), and an auto-escalation if a decision slips past the SLA.
GAP N Volunteer resourcing not acknowledged

The policy assumes assignable IT resources with predictable capacity. In practice a significant share of delivery capacity is likely volunteer. Nothing covers what happens when a resource becomes unavailable mid-build, or how capacity is realistically forecast at the Permit to Build gate.

Recommendation: Add a volunteer capacity plan as a required artifact at PTX-3 (Permit to Build). Define minimum availability thresholds and contingency when key volunteers become unavailable.
GAP O No emergency / event-driven fast path

§12 allows exceptions "approved by ITC" with no criteria or timeline. Festival and event deadlines are immovable and real. Without a defined compressed path, urgent work will simply bypass the policy — and once it happens once, it happens routinely.

Recommendation: Define a formal emergency path with specific criteria (e.g. immovable event deadline ≤30 days, Lite or Standard tier, pre-documented risks). Allow PTX-1 through PTX-3 to be combined in a single 48-hour ITC review. PTX-4 (Rollout) and PTX-5 (Close) still required — no skip.

High Priority

GAP C Support model genuinely unclear

§3 says operational business-side support sits with Departments; §4.4 says IT transitions to a "production support group" after hypercare. These aren't reconciled. Who handles L1 user questions? L2 functional configurations? L3 technical issues? What are the hypercare exit criteria and duration?

Recommendation: Define a 3-tier support model (L1/L2/L3) with owners for each tier, and specify hypercare duration and exit criteria in §4.4.
GAP D PTX-2 and PTX-3 naming ambiguity (v1.0) RESOLVED IN V0.3

§4.1 grants approval to proceed to requirements. §4.2 is titled "Permit to Design, Estimate, and Plan" but its body describes requirements elicitation — then grants approval to proceed to design and planning. §4.3 then says IT produces the technical design (which §4.2 just authorized). Recommendation: Either rename §4.2 to "Permit to Define Requirements" or explicitly move design work into §4.3's pre-work description to match the naming.
GAP E P0 classification has no defined consequence

§8 explains how ITC decides P0, but not what P0 actually gets you. Does a P0 skip gates? Jump the queue? Get dedicated resources? If a P0 still walks all four gates at normal speed, the label is meaningless. Priority tiers below P0 (P1/P2/P3) and a published backlog view are also missing.

Recommendation: Define what P0 designation means operationally (dedicated resources, expedited gate reviews, senior sponsor assignment) and create a published priority tier model (P0–P3).
GAP L No benefits realization / PTX-5 missing (v1.0) RESOLVED IN V0.3

§7 requires business value and expected benefits at intake, but nothing ever checks whether they materialized. The feedback loop that makes future estimates better doesn't exist.

Addressed in v0.3: PTX-5 "Permit to Close" is now §6.5 of the Current Policy — a post-implementation review at 60–90 days, reported to ITC, closing the benefits feedback loop.
