BAPS IT Stage Gate Process

8-stage software delivery lifecycle for volunteer IT teams

What is the Stage Gate Process?

A structured sequence of stages and decision points that every IT project must pass through — from the initial idea to production closure. Each gate is a formal review where evidence is assessed and a Go / Kill / Hold / Recycle decision is made. No stage can begin until the previous gate has formally passed.

Exit Point A

Before Stage 1

Department has its own capable team. They self-manage the project but must still follow central governance: security policies and architecture standards.

Exit Point B

After Stage 3 — Requirements done

BAPS BAs help the department define requirements, then the department exits to issue an RFP to an external vendor for the build phase.

Exit Point C

After Stage 5 — Estimate done

IT's estimate exceeds the department's timeline or budget. The department finds an external solution. IT is never pressured to rush and compromise quality.

Industry Context — Cooper Stage-Gate® vs BAPS 8-Stage

The BAPS model is grounded in Cooper's Stage-Gate® framework (the global industry standard) and extended for a volunteer nonprofit IT organization.

Cooper Stage-Gate® (Industry Standard)

1
DiscoveryIdentify opportunities, generate ideas
2
ScopingQuick preliminary assessment
3
Build Business CaseDetailed investigation, market research, technical feasibility
4
DevelopmentBuild the solution
5
Testing & LaunchValidate and deploy

BAPS 8-Stage Model

1
Project ProposalMaps to Discovery + early Scoping. ITC/BOD gate.
2
Project InitiationMaps to Scoping — formalizes team and governance.
3
RequirementsMaps to Build Business Case — BRD + FRD split is a BAPS extension.
4–5
Technical Design + EstimateCooper combines these; BAPS separates design from costing — reduces estimation surprises.
6–9
Dev + UAT + Launch + ClosureMaps to Testing & Launch. BAPS adds formal Closure and Lessons Learned loop.

BAPS Extensions Beyond Cooper

BRD / FRD Split
Cooper treats requirements as one stage. BAPS splits BRD (what/why) from FRD (how) with a formal gate between them — preventing design from starting on incomplete logic.
Exit Points A / B / C
Cooper assumes IT always builds. BAPS provides three structured off-ramps that give departments flexibility while preventing IT burnout from unrealistic expectations.
Readiness Overlap Model
Cooper gates are sequential. BAPS stages overlap: prerequisites for the next stage begin while the current stage is running, preventing stalls (e.g., finding a Solution Architect before Stage 3 ends).
Volunteer RACI
Cooper assumes dedicated staff. BAPS RACI accounts for limited volunteer availability, rotating team members, and the need to distribute accountability across a semi-professional governance structure.
Formal Closure Stage
Cooper's model ends at Launch. BAPS adds Project Closure with formal documentation archival, access revocation, and a Lessons Learned loop that feeds back into process improvements.
Defect Severity Classification
Cooper focuses on business gates. BAPS adds a P0–P3 defect severity framework to formalize what must be resolved before UAT sign-off, preventing "go-live with known issues" disputes.

Glossary

Every abbreviation used in the stage gate process, RACI matrices, and Jira templates — defined in plain language.

RGC
Regional Governance Committee
Central IT governance body. Reviews proposals, coordinates resources, and oversees project quality.
ITC
IT Committee
Senior committee that approves or defers project proposals and allocates IT resources. Reports to BOD.
BOD
Board of Directors
Executive governance authority. Escalation point when ITC cannot reach a decision within the defined SLA.
EAB
Enterprise Architecture Board
Technical standards body. Responsible for architecture sign-offs and integration decisions. Engage via RGC at Stage 2 start.
FPO
Functional Product Owner
Business-side owner of the product. Responsible for requirement sign-offs and UAT approval. Typically a department lead (Sanyojak).
BT
Business Team
Department stakeholders, SMEs, and end-users representing the business side of the project.
PM
Project Manager
Accountable for overall delivery, timelines, communications, and gate progression.
PM-Admin
Project Manager Admin
Responsible for process administration, ALM setup, MS Teams setup, and compliance tracking.
BA
Business Analyst
Responsible for requirements elicitation, BRD, FRD, and traceability matrices.
SA
Solution / Systems Architect
Designs technical architecture, leads POCs, reviews design, and signs off implementation quality.
TL
Technical Lead
Leads developers, enforces code standards, approves packages, and is accountable for code quality.
DES
Designer (UI/UX)
Creates LFD and HFD. Must comply with the BAPS Design System.
QA
Quality Analyst
Writes and executes test scenarios, test cases, performance tests, and regression tests.
SEC
Security / Cybersecurity Team
Conducts VAPT. Engage 3 weeks before go-live. Issues VAPT Certificate within 5 business days of final build.
NDC
National Development Committee
National-level oversight body. Includes engineering leads and other track leads.
SME
Subject Matter Expert
Domain expert from the business team who provides detailed knowledge during requirements workshops.
BRD
Business Requirements Document
High-level "What, Why, For Whom." Must be signed off before FRD writing begins.
FRD
Functional Requirements Document
Detailed "How." Business logic, data rules, edge cases, integrations. Signed off after BRD+LFD.
LFD
Low Fidelity Design
Wireframes/mockups at a conceptual level. Signed off together with BRD.
HFD
High Fidelity Design
Pixel-level Figma designs. Must comply with BAPS Design System. Signed off together with FRD.
TDD
Technical Design Document
Covers full architecture, component design, API contracts, data models, and integrations.
CR
Change Request
Any scope, requirement, or timeline change after Gate 3 close. Must be formally logged and approved.
RACI
Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed
R = does the work · A = signs off · C = input required · I = notified of outcome
ALM
Application Lifecycle Management
Tool (e.g., HawkEye) used to track stories, sprints, test cases, and project artefacts.
VAPT
Vulnerability Assessment and Penetration Testing
Security testing by SEC team. Round 1 before UAT; final round before go-live.
UAT
User Acceptance Testing
Business-team-led testing in a production-like environment before go-live.
POC
Proof of Concept
Time-boxed technical experiment to validate a high-risk design decision before committing to development.
SDLC
Software Development Life Cycle
The end-to-end process for planning, building, testing, and deploying software. The stage gate IS the BAPS SDLC.
SLA
Service Level Agreement
A defined time or performance commitment. E.g., ITC review = 15 business days; design review = 3 business days.
RFP
Request for Proposal
Formal document issued to external vendors when a department chooses Exit Point B.
CI/CD
Continuous Integration / Continuous Deployment
Automated pipeline. Enforces code quality gate: Grade A / 90% minimum. Fails on unapproved packages.
PII
Personally Identifiable Information
Personal data about individuals. Projects storing PII must follow data security requirements.
MOM
Minutes of Meeting
Written record of decisions, action items, and owners. Uploaded to MS Teams within 2 business days.
TPO
Technical Product Owner
Technical point of contact for a dependent system or third-party integration. Responsible for providing API credentials, integration support, and change notifications.
RTM
Requirements Traceability Matrix
Document mapping every BRD requirement → FRD section → Test Case → Result. Created in Stage 3 (BRD→FRD mapping) and extended in Stage 6 (FRD→Test Cases). 100% coverage required before gate closure.
RAID
Risks, Assumptions, Issues, Dependencies
Project management log tracking all four risk-related categories. Updated at every sprint retrospective and reviewed at gate closures.
WSR
Weekly Status Report
Regular stakeholder update shared via MS Teams. Covers RAG status, completed items, upcoming milestones, risks, and issues.
NFR
Non-Functional Requirement
System quality attribute requirements (performance, security, scalability, availability, reliability) captured in BRD and refined in FRD. SLAs defined in Stage 3 and tested in Stage 6.
